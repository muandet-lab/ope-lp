# Experiments section, draft continuation (2026-11-11)

We have shown in theory how our framework not only can incorporate existing assumptions in the partial identification literature as linear constraints but also can leverage the strengths of point estimation approaches. We conduct experiments on synthetic and real-world data to show (i) how our framework can be operationalised in practice, (ii) that as more constraints are added we can tighten the bounds on the policy value, and (iii) that when relevant values in the constraints have to be estimated, our estimated bounds converge to the true bounds of the policy value.

## Setup

**Point estimator and support split.** We build on OffCEM (Saito et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art estimator for large action spaces. OffCEM assumes local correctness: within a user-defined cluster of actions, its two-step reward model f = g + h makes the same error for every action. Following the OffCEM experiments we use its synthetic generator (1,000 actions, 200 users, 50 generating clusters, 8,000 logged rounds) and two extreme-classification datasets, EUR-Lex 4K (3,956 labels) and Wiki10-31K (5,501 labels after the authors' filter), each with 300 users and 8,000 rounds. A (user, action) pair is *supported* if the target policy puts at most 100 times the logging probability on it; the supported part of the value is estimated by OffCEM (IPS and DR are reported alongside), and every other pair enters the finite LP. On the synthetic data the LP region is the 50 percent of actions the logger never plays plus the target's heavily preferred actions, 0.9 of the target's mass; on the real data it is exactly each user's favourite label.

**Constraints.** The LP of Section 3 is instantiated with (1) the outcome range, (2) relaxed local correctness |mu(u,a) - f(u,a) - b(u,c)| <= epsilon around OffCEM's own reward model, and (3) a band |b - b_hat| <= eta on the per-cluster correction; these three are the baseline. We then add three restrictions from the partial-identification literature as further linear constraints: (4) Lipschitz smoothness of the mean reward across users for the same action, |mu(u,a) - mu(v,a)| <= L d(x_u, x_v), imposed by cutting planes and anchored on logged reference intervals; (5) cluster-specific bounds, the 5th-95th percentile range of logged reference intervals within the action's cluster; (6) favourite-action dominance, mu(u,a) <= mu(u,a*) for the target's preferred action a*, with a lower anchor from the user's logged rewards.

**Estimated quantities.** Every value in the constraints is estimated from the logs. epsilon_max is calibrated from within-cluster contrasts of logged residuals (twice the estimated spread of true means around the model after the shared correction); eta = epsilon; L is the 95th percentile of noise-corrected same-action slopes across user pairs (2.97 on the synthetic data, 0.06 on EUR-Lex, 0.14 on Wiki); reference intervals are logged means with a z = 3.5 margin, or exact Clopper-Pearson intervals for binary rewards; the reward model is screened to the range of rewards logged in the action's cluster. We report the bounds at three tolerances around the calibrated epsilon_max, floor(epsilon_max) - 1, floor, + 1 on the synthetic reward scale and epsilon_max x {0.75, 1, 1.25} on [0, 1]. Constraints (4)-(6) are first imposed exactly; if the LP is infeasible, which signals that the logged evidence contradicts the reward model, every extra constraint is relaxed by the smallest t on a fixed ladder that restores feasibility, and t is reported with the bound. Constraints (1)-(3) are never relaxed.

## (i) Operationalising the framework: what each source of extrapolation contributes

Two ingredients inform an unsupported reward: OffCEM's reward model, which transfers information across actions within a cluster, and Lipschitz smoothness, which transfers it across users for the same action. On the synthetic data with K-means clusters, the range alone gives [-27.1, 27.3]; local correctness at the calibrated epsilon = 9 narrows this to [-15.4, 17.6], Lipschitz alone to [-12.3, 16.4], and the two together to [-10.9, 13.8]. Each source contributes after the other is in place. With the generating clusters local correctness is the stronger of the two, [-9.6, 16.9] against [-12.3, 16.8], and the combination reaches [-7.1, 12.3]. All four LPs are feasible and all contain the true value 5.95. On the extreme-classification data the range alone already gives [0.01, 0.95] on EUR-Lex and [0.00, 0.96] on Wiki; Lipschitz adds nothing, since the estimated L is near zero and the logged references are single observations, and local correctness removes 0.03 from the top on EUR-Lex.

## (ii) Tightening with more constraints

Figure S3 adds constraints (4), (5), (6) cumulatively on the synthetic data with K-means clusters. At epsilon = 9 the baseline [-15.4, 17.6] becomes [-10.9, 13.8] with Lipschitz, [-9.1, 12.1] with cluster-specific bounds, and [-1.4, 12.0] with favourite-action dominance; the bound shrinks by two thirds and the true value remains inside at every step. Cluster-specific bounds act on the upper end, dominance on the lower end. Strikingly, the same chain without local correctness ends at [-0.8, 12.3], exactly feasible: once smoothness, cluster bounds and dominance are imposed, OffCEM's reward model adds little on this world. [Figure S4c: the same at OffCEM scale for four clusterings.] At scale every bound contains the true value 7.48, and the reported tolerance t exposes where the reward model is wrong: under the generating and spectral clusterings the full constraint set holds at t <= 8 and halves the baseline width; under K-means and agglomerative clusterings dominance requires t = 16, because the two-step model under-predicts a handful of favourite actions by 20 to 30 reward units, and the LP refuses a tighter tolerance rather than returning an interval that excludes the truth. On the real data all constraints are exactly feasible and the bounds are wide, [0.10, 0.92] on EUR-Lex and [0.08, 0.96] on Wiki at epsilon_max: the logger shows each favourite label at most once, so the logged-data constraints carry almost no information, and the LP reports that honestly. [Favourite-action dominance is true by construction for the epsilon-greedy targets of these experiments; for a general target it is a belief about the target's quality, and the reported t is the regret margin at which it holds.]
